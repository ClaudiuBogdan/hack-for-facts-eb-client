# Who Decides and When?

## A Process with Multiple Actors

The budget is the result of a process with multiple steps and multiple actors. It is prepared by the executive side of the municipality and approved by the deliberative side. If you want to understand when public input still matters, this distinction helps most.

## Who prepares it

The mayor and the executive apparatus set the direction and turn priorities into a budget draft. In practice, the economic department translates those priorities into amounts and spending categories.

In our running example, this is where the project to renovate a school and equip a lab first appears in a budget form.

## Who approves it

Local council members discuss the draft by domain, and the council gives it final form through a vote. This is where you see whether the proposed amount stays, changes, or disappears.

In short: the executive side prepares, the deliberative side approves. These roles are connected, but they are not the same.

## When your voice matters

Citizens and civic groups do not vote the budget, but they can still matter before the vote. After the draft is published, they can submit observations, ask questions, and apply public pressure while the document can still change.

If you arrive after approval, your role shifts toward monitoring: watching execution, rectifications, and whether promises were actually followed through.

<ExpandableHint label="Legal basis and procedural notes">
- [Law no. 273/2006 on local public finance](https://legislatie.just.ro/Public/DetaliiDocument/73527), Article 39, lays out the path of the local budget draft from completion to approval by the deliberative authority.
- [Law no. 52/2003 on decision-making transparency](https://legislatie.just.ro/Public/DetaliiDocument/41571) sets general rules for prior information, citizen consultation, and participation in public meetings.
</ExpandableHint>
