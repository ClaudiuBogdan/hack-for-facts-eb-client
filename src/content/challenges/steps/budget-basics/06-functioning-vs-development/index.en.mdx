# Functioning vs Development

## Running a Service or Changing It

When you compare amounts in a budget, one of the most useful questions is: does the money keep a service running, or does it visibly change that service?

## What keeps a service running

The functioning section covers the spending that keeps a service active day by day:

- salaries
- utilities
- cleaning
- routine maintenance

In the case of a school, this includes heating, cleaning, supplies, and small repairs.

## What changes the service

The development section covers spending that changes infrastructure, quality, or capacity:

- new construction
- renovations
- equipment and fittings

Renovating a school and equipping a lab do not just keep the school open. They change what the service can offer going forward.

> **Important:** A large education budget does not automatically mean a strong investment effort. You can have high running costs and still very little money for modernization.

## Why the difference matters

Not all amounts are equally easy to move. If you cut functioning costs, the service may suffer immediately. Investments are more visible to the public, but they are often the first to be postponed.

This distinction helps you ask better questions: are you trying to protect basic service delivery, defend an investment line, or ask why a project remained only on paper?

<ExpandableHint label="Legal basis and procedural notes">
- [Law no. 273/2006 on local public finance](https://legislatie.just.ro/Public/DetaliiDocument/73527), Article 2, defines the `functioning section` as the mandatory part of the budget for current expenditure and the `development section` as the complementary part for capital expenditure and development policies.
- In the same law, Article 26 provides that capital expenditure is included under each budget chapter and that programs are approved as annexes to the budget.
- Investments may appear in a separate annex and can span multiple years.
</ExpandableHint>
