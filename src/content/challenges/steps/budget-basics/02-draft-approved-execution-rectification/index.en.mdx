# Draft, Approved, Execution, Rectification

## Why the Document Stage Matters

When you open a budget document, the first useful question is: are you looking at an intention, a formal decision, or what actually happened? The same amount can mean something very different depending on the stage.

## Not all documents say the same thing

- The **draft budget** can still change.
- The **approved budget** is the version voted by the local council.
- **Budget execution** shows what was actually paid.
- **Rectification** changes the approved budget during the year.

In short: the budget is the plan, execution is the reality, and rectification shows where priorities shifted along the way.

## How the same project changes

Imagine a project to renovate a school and equip a lab:

- in the **draft**, it appears as a proposal and can still be challenged or adjusted
- in the **approved budget**, it becomes an official allocation
- in **execution**, you see whether real payments appeared for works or equipment
- in **rectification**, the project can gain money, lose money, or be postponed

> **Note:** An approved project is not guaranteed. Only execution confirms whether the money was actually spent.

## The useful question

When you see a budget document, ask: can this still be influenced, or can I only monitor it? For participation, the draft stage matters most. For accountability, execution and rectifications matter most.

Once you make this distinction, it becomes clearer who decides and when public input still matters.

<ExpandableHint label="Legal basis and procedural notes">
- [Law no. 273/2006 on local public finance](https://legislatie.just.ro/Public/DetaliiDocument/73527), Article 39, requires the local budget draft to be finalized within 15 days from the publication of the state budget law.
- The same article allows residents to submit objections within 15 days from the publication or display of the draft, and the deliberative authority approves the budget after reviewing them.
- Article 39 also sets a maximum term of 45 days from the publication of the state budget law for approving the local budget.
</ExpandableHint>
