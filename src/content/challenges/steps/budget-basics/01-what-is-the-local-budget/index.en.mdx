# What Is the Local Budget?

The street you walk on, the local school, street lighting, transport. They all cost money. The local budget is the yearly plan through which a municipality shows where its money comes from and how it chooses to spend it.

## What it includes

A local budget has two sides:

- **revenues**: money coming in (taxes, local fees, transfers from central government)
- **spending**: money going out (salaries, repairs, new projects)

In simple terms, the budget answers two questions: how much money the municipality starts with and what it chooses to do with it during the year.

## Why it matters

A municipality's money is limited. If it chooses to renovate a school, that same money can no longer go to roads or green spaces. The budget is not just a table of numbers. It is where you see what was treated as a priority and what was postponed.

## What you can read in a budget

When you open a budget, you are not only seeing amounts. You are seeing what stays in operation, what gets developed, what gets delayed, and what is treated as urgent. With that in mind, the next step is to see that not all budget documents show the same thing.

<ExpandableHint label="Legal basis and procedural notes">
- [Law no. 273/2006 on local public finance](https://legislatie.just.ro/Public/DetaliiDocument/73527) sets the general framework for drafting, approving, executing, and reporting local budgets.
- In the same law, Article 26 describes the general structure of the local budget, while Article 39 regulates the completion and approval of the local budget draft.
- Taxes and contributions do not flow directly into a single service. Money is pooled into a common budget and then distributed across different priorities.
</ExpandableHint>
