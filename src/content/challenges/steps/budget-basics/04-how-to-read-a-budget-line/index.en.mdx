# How to Read One Budget Line

You do not need to know the whole budget classification system to begin. It is enough to understand a few markers that help you locate an amount and understand what it represents.

## What a budget line is

A budget line is the place in the budget where you see a concrete amount tied to a domain and a type of spending. It does not tell you everything through a single code. In practice, you need to look at several elements together.

## Why it matters

If you only say "I want more money for schools," you stay very general. If you can show where the amount appears, what it relates to, and what kind of spending it is, you can ask a clear question. Reading a budget line helps you move from a general complaint to a question about a real allocation.

## What to look for and how it looks in practice

If you are tracking the expansion or modernization of a school, here are the markers you look for in the budget:

- **the functional code**: shows the domain. For example, `65.02.04.01` points to lower secondary education.
- **the economic code**: shows the type of spending. For example, `71.01` points to fixed assets, and `71.01.01` points to construction.
- **the amount**: the allocated value for that category.
- **the section**: shows whether the amount falls under functioning or development.
- **the annex or investment program**: this is where you find the concrete project name. In the main budget body, labels are often standardized. Names like "School extension" or "School modernization" usually appear in the investment annex.

> **Note:** In many local budgets, amounts are shown in `thousand lei`, not directly in lei. That means `1,250` can mean `1,250,000 lei`. If you see the same amount in multiple places, it is often the move from summary to detail, not a real duplication.

Now that you have a clearer reference point, the next step is to see how the same amount can be read through two lenses: the domain and the type of spending.

<ExpandableHint label="Legal basis and procedural notes">
- [Law no. 273/2006 on local public finance](https://legislatie.just.ro/Public/DetaliiDocument/73527), Article 26, shows that local budget expenditure is structured by parts, chapters, subchapters, titles, articles, lines, and paragraphs, as applicable.
- In the same law, the public investment program is presented as an annex to the budget, within the development section, with both financial and non-financial information for investment objectives.
- The public-finance indicator classification is approved through [Order of the Ministry of Public Finance no. 1954/2005](https://www.mfinante.gov.ro/static/10/Mfp/buget/sitebuget/clasificatii/Anexanr2_09012025.xls), which provides the basis for the functional and economic codes used in budget forms.
